# 1. Introduction

The first week focused on understanding the Urban Heat Island (UHI) phenomenon and laying a strong foundation for the project. The primary objective was to clearly define the problem, understand the requirements, study existing research, evaluate available technologies, and prepare a structured development roadmap.

Since Boreas-NEXUS is a multidisciplinary project involving Geographic Information Systems (GIS), Remote Sensing, Machine Learning, and Web Technologies, the initial phase emphasized planning rather than implementation. This ensures that subsequent development follows a modular, scalable, and maintainable architecture.

---

# 2. Problem Identification

## Problem Statement

Urban Heat Island (UHI) is a phenomenon where urban regions experience significantly higher temperatures than surrounding rural areas due to rapid urbanization, increased impervious surfaces, reduced vegetation, and anthropogenic activities.

Although satellite imagery and climate datasets are widely available, many existing solutions only identify heat hotspots and lack integrated tools to:

- Analyze the causes of heat accumulation.
- Predict future temperature variations.
- Simulate cooling interventions.
- Support planners with data-driven recommendations.

The project aims to address these limitations by developing an intelligent platform capable of identifying urban heat hotspots, analyzing contributing factors, predicting thermal patterns, evaluating mitigation strategies, and assisting decision-makers through an interactive web application.

# 4. Requirement Analysis

## Functional Requirements

The system should be able to:

- Collect satellite and GIS datasets.
- Preprocess spatial datasets.
- Generate land surface temperature maps.
- Detect urban heat hotspots.
- Analyze factors affecting heat distribution.
- Predict future thermal conditions.
- Simulate multiple cooling interventions.
- Display results on an interactive GIS dashboard.
- Generate reports and recommendations.

## Non-Functional Requirements

The system should:

- Be modular and scalable.

- Produce reproducible analytical results.
- Provide responsive visualization.

- Support future integration of advanced AI models.

## Key Findings

Existing solutions generally focus on one or two aspects of Urban Heat Island analysis, such as hotspot detection or temperature prediction.

However, few systems provide a unified workflow combining:

- Data acquisition
- Heat hotspot identification
- Driver analysis
- Prediction
- Scenario simulation
- Decision support

This identified an opportunity to develop an integrated platform capable of supporting urban planners throughout the decision-making process.
